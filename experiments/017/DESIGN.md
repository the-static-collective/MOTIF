## Measurement procedure

Each *invented* evidence source declares a stable upstream origin, a synthetic per-query cost, and an independently adjudicated synthetic calibration count. Beta(2,2) yields a posterior source-reliability mean under the declared imaginary sampling regime. Reporting below 50% correctness can still provide information when treated as an anti-reliable signal. None of this authenticates real people, networks, source honesty, or external provenance.

The declared latent binary state is: expected-cost method is lower-loss or worst-case method is lower-loss. The planner sees ONLY a declared prior and source-calibration descriptors, **never the simulator's hidden latent true state**.

Its options are:
- STOP with HOLD or an inert method review candidate;
- REQUEST ONE source observation;
- REQUEST TWO INDEPENDENT source observations in parallel.

The finite batch selector exactly enumerates all 0/1/2-probe source sets under budget and minimizes **query cost + expected posterior decision risk**. Each of the 2^n possible response combinations is explicitly integrated. The terminal decision compares candidate incorrect-method loss with the declared cost of HOLD. A recommendation to change method is never adopted automatically.

### Strong classical benchmark

The separate exact **adaptive Bayesian two-step dynamic program** sees the same prior, sources, calibrations, budget, and loss model. It may observe the first report before deciding whether to request a second. Because it is strictly more flexible than a fixed simultaneous bundle, the batch optimizer should **tie or lose** to the classical sequential DP on its own model assumptions. This is evidence of working finite decision logic, not invention of Bayesian experimental design or superiority.

## Negative cases / refusals

1. Two modest independent sources: two cheap concurrent questions are more valuable together than either individually; asking is still only a proposal.
2. A nearly reliable source: one question is worthwhile; a costly second is unnecessary.
3. Too-expensive probes: HOLD.
4. Forwarded copies of one origin: cannot be treated as two independent witnesses. Duplicates count once; contradictions within the same declared origin HOLD.
5. Anti-reliable calibrated synthetic source: reverse the likelihood; no inference of malicious intent.
6. Missing original source: National Treasure Question Box HOLD, and Dogram is not called.
7. Independently disagreeing sources: return HOLD rather than manufacture certainty.
8. **Unannounced inversion of a previously calibrated source:** a counterexample that may fool the Bayesian method. A declared historical calibration is not a guarantee about the next environment.
9. Altered cartridge, outside-scope report, missing calibration, budget abuse: HOLD or reject.
