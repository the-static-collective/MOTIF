## Generated conjectures versus supplied candidates

The study's *input* contains no prewritten bridge hypotheses. For each of three source graph records, the program enumerates all connected two-edge substructures, normalizes node labels, and intersects the resulting signatures. Two examples of **derived** canonical expressions produced in the runs are:

- **0+1|0+2** — two outward positive directed edges from a common vertex.
- **0+1|1-2** — a positive directed edge followed by a negative directed edge.

These symbols are canonical forms of observed local subgraphs, not names of predetermined causal laws. Each is paired with exact public input edge references and a prospective question about an intervention. The active synthetic effect is determined by the hidden generator and not passed to the hypothesis constructor.

A deliberately weak lexical comparator would choose recurring surface words such as "blue ribbon", which are irrelevant to the assigned synthetic effect. **A strong classical graph enumerator with exactly the same signed-edge grammar plus the same Fisher tests would obtain identical questions and classifications.** This shows that the new work is composition of a pipeline, not an algorithmic breakthrough.

## Posthoc confounding challenge

A later, clearly marked **POSTHOC** control simulated latent Z causing both an observational exposure and the measured outcome, without any causal effect of intervening on the exposure.

Both generated signatures displayed large apparent observational contrasts in each of the three strands (roughly 0.70–0.89 in one control run). Yet the observational records were explicitly typed OBSERVATIONAL_NOT_RANDOMIZED, and the intervention analyzer returned:

**HOLD_OBSERVATIONAL_DATA_NOT_AN_INTERVENTION**

for each generated candidate. Tiny p-values in the survey are printed in scientific notation rather than rounded to 0. The test cannot pass a high correlation off as an independently randomized cause. This guard does not rule out forged experimental records or invalid scientific sampling in a real deployment.
