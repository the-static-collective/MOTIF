The Reciprocal Apprentice

Two imperfect reasoners, one inspectable method. Teach a finite machine through examples; learn a technique for choosing better questions; then try a new question without hints.

Offline, local to this page. No model weights, account, analytics, uploads, network calls, remote actions or automatic persistence. Synthetic four-row Boolean laboratory, not a claim of general intelligence or measured human learning.

01 · Human baseline (answer before the lesson)

Which question would you ask?

Five possible rules could explain a hidden situation: ZERO, A, AND, XNOR, IMPLIES. You may ask about one pair of conditions, and hear a yes/no answer. Which single test best separates these possibilities?

ZERO = never; A = first condition; AND = both; XNOR = conditions equal; IMPLIES = either the first is false or the second is true.

02 · MOTIF teaches a transferable method

Ask the question that divides the hypotheses.

Instead of picking the most familiar example, imagine each candidate rule making its own prediction. A useful test divides those predictions into competing groups. With equally likely rules, select the test minimizing the expected number of rules left after you hear its answer.

E[remaining] = (count(no)² + count(yes)²) / total

That is an ordinary classical information-gain-style heuristic, not an invented MOTIF discovery. Try teaching the machine before tackling a new challenge.

03 · Person teaches MOTIF

Give examples. Correct the machine.

Choose a secret rule for the exercise. The inference engine starts with eight alternatives, and only receives the 0/1 observations you choose to submit.

Start to let MOTIF select its first informative question.

A contradictory statement is preserved as a contested report and triggers HOLD. Replacing an answer is a distinct human-authorized event; the original is not erased from the log.

Registering an operator does not infer its semantics or grant authority. It simply extends the finite search vocabulary with your four declared outputs.

05 · What the results actually mean

  
Two learners; different evidence

  
No measurements yet. Individual responses are not proof of durable human learning.

  
The machine experiment tests exact finite rule elimination. The human exercise measures one choice before and after a single explanation, with different engineer-authored problems. Improved post-score is at most a hint of near-transfer; it is not a controlled human study. A strong classical optimizer makes exactly the same selections as our machine here.
